Knee Replacement Cost in Mumbai: What Actually Drives the Price

A plain explanation of what you are paying for, which choices move the number, and how insurance is handled at Kokilaben Dhirubhai Ambani Hospital.

The cost of a total knee replacement is not a single figure — it is a package built from the implant chosen, the surgical technique, the ward category, the length of stay and your medical complexity. Understanding those five components tells you far more than a headline price does.

Almost every patient asks about cost at the first consultation, and it is a fair question. What follows is an honest account of how a knee replacement is priced in a Mumbai tertiary hospital, what genuinely changes the number, and where patients are most often caught out. Dr. Joshi's team gives every patient a written estimate before admission, so there are no surprises on the day.

What is included in a knee replacement package

A quoted knee replacement package at a hospital of Kokilaben's standard is normally an all-in figure covering the elements below. It is worth checking each one against any quotation you are comparing, because packages are not defined identically across hospitals.

  • Surgeon's professional fee — the operating surgeon and the assisting surgical team.
  • Anaesthetist's fee — pre-anaesthetic assessment, the anaesthetic itself and post-operative pain management.
  • Operating theatre charges — laminar-flow theatre time, sterile consumables, instrumentation and theatre staff.
  • The implant — usually the single largest line item, and the one that varies most.
  • Hospital stay — room charges for the expected length of stay, nursing care and routine medication.
  • Physiotherapy — inpatient rehabilitation, which begins the morning after surgery.
  • Routine investigations — pre-operative blood work, ECG, chest X-ray and post-operative check films.

What is usually not included

Costs that sit outside a standard package are the ones that surprise patients, so it is worth asking about them explicitly when you take your estimate.

  • Treatment of pre-existing medical conditions that need optimising before surgery — uncontrolled diabetes, cardiac assessment, anaemia correction.
  • An extended stay beyond the packaged number of days, if your recovery needs it.
  • Intensive care, should a medical complication require it.
  • Outpatient physiotherapy after discharge, which typically continues for several weeks.
  • Walking aids, knee braces, compression stockings and home equipment.
  • Follow-up consultations and X-rays after the initial post-operative reviews.

The five factors that genuinely change the price

1. The implant you and your surgeon choose

This is the biggest single variable. A standard cobalt-chrome and polyethylene total knee sits at the base of the range. Above it sit implants chosen for specific clinical reasons — and each carries a different price.

  • Oxinium (oxidised zirconium) — a ceramic-surfaced femoral component that is far more scratch-resistant and smoother than cobalt-chrome, producing substantially less polyethylene wear. Chosen for younger, more active patients and for those with metal allergy. Dr. Joshi is an on-panel specialist for this system.
  • High-flex systems — engineered for flexion to around 150–155°, for patients who need to sit cross-legged, kneel for prayer or squat.
  • Gender-specific components — sized to the narrower female knee and its different trochlear geometry, to avoid bony overhang.
  • Constrained or hinged systems — required where there is severe deformity, instability or bone loss, and considerably more expensive.

Implant choice is a clinical decision before it is a financial one. A 45-year-old who will load the joint for decades and a 78-year-old with modest demands are not well served by the same component. Dr. Joshi discusses the reasoning openly at consultation — see the knee replacement page for the full range of systems he uses.

2. One knee or both

A bilateral procedure — replacing both knees under one anaesthetic — costs more in absolute terms than a single knee, but appreciably less than two separate admissions. It also means one anaesthetic, one hospital stay and one rehabilitation period. It is not right for everyone: bilateral surgery places greater physiological demand on the patient, and suitability depends on cardiac and respiratory fitness, haemoglobin and age.

3. Conventional or robotic-assisted

Robotic-assisted knee replacement using the VELYS platform costs modestly more than conventional surgery, reflecting the technology and the disposable instrumentation involved. It does not require a pre-operative CT scan — the VELYS system maps the knee live during surgery — so it avoids the scan cost and radiation that older robotic platforms required. The robotic knee replacement guide explains the technique in detail.

4. Room category and length of stay

Ward, twin-sharing, single room and suite categories carry markedly different daily rates, and the room category often also determines the rate applied to other package elements. A typical uncomplicated total knee replacement involves a stay of four to five days. Complex or revision cases run longer — five to ten days is usual.

5. Your medical complexity

A patient with well-controlled health and straightforward anatomy is priced at the base of the range. Significant deformity, prior knee surgery, obesity, poorly controlled diabetes, or a cardiac or renal condition requiring peri-operative management all add cost — through longer theatre time, specialist implants, additional investigations or a longer stay.

Health insurance and cashless treatment

Total knee replacement is a covered procedure under the great majority of Indian health insurance policies, and Kokilaben Dhirubhai Ambani Hospital has cashless arrangements with most major insurers and TPAs. In practice the process runs as follows.

Points worth checking with your insurer

Ask specifically about: the waiting period for joint replacement (commonly two to four years from policy inception); any implant sub-limit or room-rent cap, since a room-rent cap can proportionately reduce every other reimbursed item; whether robotic assistance is covered or treated as an excluded upgrade; and whether a bilateral procedure is treated as one claim or two.

Is robotic knee replacement worth the extra cost?

This is a judgement, not a formula, and the honest answer depends on how many years you expect the implant to serve you. The argument for paying more is that alignment accuracy drives implant survival: poor component positioning is the leading cause of early failure, and revision surgery is significantly more expensive, more difficult and slower to recover from than the original operation.

For a patient in their eighties with modest activity demands, conventional surgery in experienced hands gives an excellent result and the marginal benefit is small. For a patient in their fifties who may need that knee to last thirty years, the calculation shifts — avoiding a single revision procedure across a lifetime more than repays the difference. Dr. Joshi will give you a direct opinion on which side of that line your case falls.

Questions worth asking before you commit

  • Is this quotation all-inclusive, and what specifically falls outside it?
  • Which implant is being proposed for me, and why that one rather than a cheaper alternative?
  • How many days does the package cover, and what is the daily rate beyond that?
  • What happens to the cost if a complication extends my stay?
  • How many knee replacements does the operating surgeon perform each year, and does he do revisions?
  • Will the consultant named in the quotation be the surgeon actually operating?

That last question matters more than most patients realise. At Kokilaben, Dr. Joshi performs the surgery himself; he does not delegate primary or revision arthroplasty. If you are weighing up surgeons rather than hospitals, the guide to choosing a joint replacement surgeon sets out the criteria that actually predict outcome.
